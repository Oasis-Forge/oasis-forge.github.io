# Privacy Policy

_Last updated: 2 November 2026_

This policy explains how **Dust Devil** ("the game"), by Oasis Forge, handles your information, on Android and iOS.

## Summary

**The game shows ads from Google AdMob, set up for children for everyone:** no personalized ads, and no advertising
ID. To show them, Google's ad library collects some technical information from your device, such as its internet
address and which ads were shown (see [Ads](#ads)). If you buy something, Unity's purchase library sends Unity a record
of the purchase and some technical information about the device (see [Buying with real money](#buying-with-real-money)).
Apart from that, **the game collects nothing about you,** unless you're 16 or over and turn on friends' scores. There
are no analytics. What the game remembers stays on your device, and nothing else leaves it unless you share it yourself, or turn on
[friends' scores](#friends-scores-16-and-over), which send your Daily Stunt result and replay, who you fly as and a friend code to
Unity, the company that runs the board.

## Information stored on your device

- Your settings: sound, music, vibration and calm motion on or off.
- Your stars, best distance and whether you've landed in each stage, the stage you played last, and the first-minute
  tips you've seen.
- Your coins, your lives and when your next life comes back, the characters, gear and snacks you've bought, who you
  fly as and what they wear, your garage's levels, the boost trails you've earned, and the worlds' rewards you've been
  given.
- The crew's camp: the upgrades you've built, and for each daredevil, how close you are (its bond), the day you last fed
  it, and whether you've found its favourite snack.
- The day's missions and how far along each is, the meters you've flown and the rank they've reached, your stickers,
  your last Daily Stunt result and how many days in a row you've landed it.
- Your replays: for your last runs and the ones you've starred, the run itself (the stage, its seed and the taps you
  made, so it can be played again), when it was played, how it ended, how far it went, and who flew it in what gear.
  Not a video: the game plays the run again from its taps.
- Purchases made with real money: their order numbers (so a purchase is never given twice), and whether ads are
  removed.
- How many runs you've played, and the run of the last ad between runs, so ads stay rare.
- Friends' scores: the birth month and year you gave when you first opened them (never sent anywhere), whether they're
  on, the player ID and sign-in token Unity gave the game, your friend code, a result waiting to go up while you're
  offline, the names you gave your friends (never sent anywhere), and your last place on the board.
- The picture you last shared (see below), in the game's cache folder, until the next share replaces it or your device
  clears the cache.

The developer has no access to any of it on your device. Deleting the game deletes it all from the device. If your
Android device backs up its apps to your Google account, Android may keep a copy of the game's save in that backup,
and bring it back when the game is installed again. Among other things, that save holds the answer to the age question
and, if you turned friends' scores on, their sign-in (the player ID and sign-in token). You can delete the backup in your Google
account's backups.

## Sharing

When you tap **Share** after a run, the game makes a picture of your crash report and a line of text with the stage,
how far you flew and your stars, and hands them to your device's share sheet. Nothing is sent anywhere unless you
choose an app and send it; what that app does with it is covered by its own privacy policy. **Copy result** puts the
line of text on your clipboard.

## Friends' scores (16 and over)

Friends' scores put your friends' Daily Stunt results next to yours. They're the game's one online feature, for players
16 and over, and they're off until you turn them on.

**The age question.** The first time you open friends' scores, the game asks for the month and year you were born. The
answer stays on your device and is never sent. If you're under 16, friends' scores stay closed, and nothing is sent;
they open by themselves in the month you turn 16.

**What goes to Unity** if you turn them on. The board is run by Unity Technologies, through its Unity Gaming Services
(sign-in, friends, Cloud Code verification and leaderboards):

- a player ID made by Unity (the game signs in anonymously: no name, email or password);
- your friend code (four random letters the game makes, with a random number Unity adds, like KFQM#4821);
- your friend list, and the friend requests you send and get;
- each day's Daily Stunt result (your first landing that day): its day, how far you flew, the points, and how many days
  in a row you've landed it, with your result before it;
- that run's replay: stage, seed, launch angle and power, tuning, simulation version, and the timing of presses and
  releases. Unity replays it to verify the result. The board's streak counts consecutive verified submissions;
- who you fly as and what they wear.

Nothing you type is sent: not the names you give your friends. No email, no location, and no advertising or device
IDs. Unity also sees your device's internet address, as every web request does.

**Who can see it.** Your code, results and who you fly as are stored at Unity, under a random player ID. The game
shows them only to your friends, the people you and they have both said yes to, and there's no public board. But
Unity's board isn't private to friends: someone with a changed copy of the game could ask Unity for it, and read the
results and looks of players who turned friends' scores on. That's why friends' scores hold nothing you type, and no
name, email or location.

Unity handles this data under its own [privacy policy](https://unity.com/legal/privacy-policy).

**Leaving.** Tap **Leave friends' scores** at the bottom of the board. The game deletes your friends and requests and
your verified score, then your Unity player. It forgets the sign-in on the phone. A score that was never verified
can't be deleted by the game, so it's cleared to nothing (no result, no details), and we delete the empty entry when
you ask. If Unity cannot be reached, or the score can't be deleted, the game keeps your account and asks you to try
leaving again online.

**Who holds it, and for how long.** Friends' scores are held at Unity, in a Unity project that Oasis Forge runs. We can
see the entries there, and we delete them when you ask. They stay while friends' scores are on, until you leave or ask
us to delete them. Deleting the game without leaving keeps them at Unity, with no way for the game to reach them again:
email us to have them deleted (see [Deleting your data](#deleting-your-data)). Unity keeps its own records under its
own retention rules: see its [privacy policy](https://unity.com/legal/privacy-policy).

## Buying with real money

The store sells coins and "Remove ads" for real money. A grown-up's question comes first. Google Play (or Apple's App
Store on iPhone) takes the payment, under its own privacy policy: the game never sees your payment details or your
account. It gets the item bought and its order number, and keeps the order numbers on your device so a purchase is
never given twice. Unity's In-App Purchasing, part of the game, passes the purchase between the game and the store.

**What Unity's purchase library sends.** Unity's In-App Purchasing sends Unity Technologies a record each time a
purchase starts, goes through, fails or is finished, and each time you restore purchases. The records are sent only
around purchases, never while you play, and we can't turn them off: they're built into Unity's library. Unity's library
may also contact Unity as the game starts, to fetch its own settings. Each record has:

- the item, its price and currency, and the store's receipt for it (its order number and the store's signature, never
  your payment details), and whether it went through or why not;
- the game's version and when it was installed, and a random ID Unity makes for this installation of the game (on
  iPhone, also the ID Apple gives the apps of one developer);
- the device's model, its operating system version, its languages, its storage size and when it was last started.

It's sent encrypted. Unity handles it under its
[Game Player and App User Privacy Policy](https://unity.com/legal/game-player-and-app-user-privacy-policy).

## Ads

The game shows ads from Google AdMob: a short one between runs now and then, and ones you can choose to watch: for a
life when you're out of lives, for a second wind after a wipeout, and for double coins on the crash report. Ads between
runs never come in your first five runs, right after a wipeout, or after you quit a run, and at most one comes every
three runs. **Remove ads** turns them off; the ads you choose stay, and only play when you tap them. There are no
banners, and every ad is Google's own, full screen, never made to look like part of the game.

**Set up for children, for everyone.** Every ad request tells Google to treat it as directed to a child, with ads rated
G (for general audiences) at most, and with personalization off. So no one gets personalized ads or remarketing, and
nothing is used to build a profile of you. The game doesn't use your device's advertising ID: it takes out the
permission that would let it, and Android's own ad services permissions too.

**What Google collects.** To show ads, count them, limit how often they show and stop fraud, Google's ad library (the
Google Mobile Ads SDK, part of the game) collects, as Google describes it:

- your device's internet (IP) address, which may be used to estimate its general location (never its precise location);
- how the ads are used: which were shown, watched or tapped, and when the game was opened;
- diagnostic information about the game and the ad library, such as how long they take to start and how they perform;
- an app set ID: a random ID Android gives the apps of one developer on a device. It isn't the advertising ID, and it's
  not shared with other developers' apps.

It's sent encrypted. Google handles it under its own [privacy policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy) (see also
[how Google uses information from apps that use its services](https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites)),
which limits how data from requests marked as directed to children is used. Google's ad library starts with the game,
even after "Remove ads", so the ads you choose are ready when you want them.

**What we get** from AdMob is totals only: how many ads were shown and what they earned. Nothing in it says who you are,
so we can't look up or delete anything Google holds about your device; Google's own privacy controls cover that.

## The game engine

Dust Devil is made with Unity, and uses no Unity Ads or Unity Analytics. Friends' scores, if you turn them on, use Unity
Gaming Services, and purchases use Unity's In-App Purchasing (see above). The Unity engine may still send Unity Technologies limited technical information about the
device, such as its model and operating system; it includes nothing you do or type in the game. Unity's
[Game Player and App User Privacy Policy](https://unity.com/legal/game-player-and-app-user-privacy-policy) explains what
Unity collects and why.

## Permissions

- **Android:** billing, so the store can sell through Google Play; internet access and network state (whether you're
  online), used by the ads, by buying and by friends' scores (only if you turn them on); vibration, for the buzz of a
  hit; and two that Google's ad library adds for its work in the background: keeping the device awake for a moment
  (wake lock) and running a short task while the game is in the background (foreground service). The game takes out
  the advertising ID permissions Google's library would add. It has no access to your location, contacts, photos or
  files.
- **iOS:** none.

## Children

The game is for a general audience, children included. Its ads are set up for children for everyone (see
[Ads](#ads)), real-money purchases ask a grown-up's question first, and there's no chat. Friends' scores, its one other
online feature, are for players 16 and over: the game asks for your birth month and year first, and players under 16
can't turn them on. Apart from what Google's ad library collects to show ads, what Unity's purchase library records
when something is bought (see [Buying with real money](#buying-with-real-money)), and friends' scores for players 16 and
over who turn them on, we don't knowingly collect information from anyone, children included.

## Deleting your data

- **On your device:** delete the game, or clear its data in your device's settings.
  A copy in your Google account's backups (see
  [Information stored on your device](#information-stored-on-your-device)) is deleted there.
- **Friends' scores, in the game:** tap **Leave friends' scores** (see [Friends' scores](#friends-scores-16-and-over)).
- **Friends' scores, without the game:** email [oasisforge.support@gmail.com](mailto:oasisforge.support@gmail.com)
  with your friend code, and we'll delete your entry, results, friends and friend code at Unity within a month.

## Your rights

Oasis Forge makes the game and is responsible for this policy. What's on your device is yours to delete. Friends'
scores, if you turn them on, sit in a Unity project that Oasis Forge runs: we can see them there, and we delete them
when you ask. What Google and Unity hold for ads and purchases is covered above. Where your country's law gives you
rights over your data (such as the GDPR in Europe and the UK, or COPPA in the US), you, or a parent for a child, can ask
us to see it, correct it or delete it, and to stop using it, by emailing us. For friends' scores, send your friend code
so we can find it at Unity. We answer within a month. You can also complain to your country's data protection
authority.

Why we use data: ads (our legitimate interest in paying for a free game, set up for children for everyone), purchases
(to give you what you bought), and friends' scores (only once you turn them on). Google and Unity may handle data in
the United States and other countries, under their own safeguards. For how long Google and Unity keep the data of ads
and purchases, see Google's [retention page](https://policies.google.com/technologies/retention) and the retention part
of Unity's [Game Player and App User Privacy Policy](https://unity.com/legal/game-player-and-app-user-privacy-policy).
