# Privacy Policy

_Last updated: 29 October 2026_

This policy explains how **Two Rooms** ("the game"), by Oasis Forge, handles your information on Android.

## Summary

**The game shows ads from Google AdMob.** To show them, Google's ad library collects some information from your device,
such as its internet address, its advertising ID and which ads were shown, and in some countries asks for your consent
first (see [Ads](#ads)). If you buy **Remove ads**, Google Play takes the payment and Unity's purchase library sends Unity
a record of the purchase (see [Buying Remove ads](#buying-remove-ads)). Apart from that, **the game collects nothing
about you.** There are no accounts, no analytics of ours, and no game server: what the game remembers stays on your
device.

## Information stored on your device

- Your progress in each case: whether it's solved, your best stars, a case left half-played (what you changed in the
  past, the clues you found, the guests you met, the codes you set and the votes you've used), and the hint lines you've
  unlocked.
- Whether you've bought **Remove ads**.
- Your settings: sound and music volume, vibration on or off, text size, reduced motion, and which tutorials you've seen.

The developer has no access to any of it. Deleting the game deletes it all from the device; **Reset progress** in the
game's Settings clears your progress (and keeps Remove ads). If your Android device backs up its apps to your Google
account, Android may keep a copy of these files in that backup and bring them back when the game is installed again;
you can delete the backup in your Google account's backups.

## Downloading chapters

Chapter 1 comes with the game. Chapters 2 to 5 download from Google Play the first time you open one of their cases,
through Google Play's own delivery service (Play Asset Delivery). That download is between your device and Google Play,
which already knows the apps installed from it: the game sends nothing of its own. Once downloaded, a chapter plays
offline.

## Ads

The game shows ads from Google AdMob at two moments only, never while you play a case:

- a full-screen ad when you leave the end card of a case you've solved, from case 3 on (cases 1 and 2 have no ads, and
  a case you lose shows none);
- a short ad you can choose to watch for a hint's second or third line, from case 3 on. The first line of every hint is
  free, and you can always keep playing instead.

There are no banners. **Remove ads** turns every ad off and makes every hint free.

**Consent.** Where the law asks for it (in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland), the game
shows Google's consent form before any ad, and you can change your choice later in the game's Settings, under
**Privacy options**.

**What Google collects.** To show ads, measure them, limit how often they show, prevent fraud and, where you have
agreed or the law allows it, personalize them, Google's ad library (the Google Mobile Ads SDK, part of the game)
collects, as Google describes it:

- your device's internet (IP) address, which may be used to estimate its general location (never its precise location);
- your device's advertising ID, and on newer Android versions the signals of Android's own ad services (ad topics and
  attribution);
- how the ads are used: which were shown, watched or tapped, and when the game was opened;
- diagnostic information about the game and the ad library, such as how they perform.

It's sent encrypted. Google handles it under its own [privacy policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy) (see also
[how Google uses information from apps that use its services](https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites)).
You can reset or delete your advertising ID, or opt out of personalized ads, in your device's settings (Settings →
Google → Ads, or Settings → Privacy → Ads on some devices).

**What we get** from AdMob is totals only: how many ads were shown and what they earned. Nothing in it says who you are,
so we can't look up or delete anything Google holds about your device; Google's own privacy controls cover that.

## Buying Remove ads

**Remove ads** is the game's one purchase, made through Google Play, which takes the payment under its own privacy
policy: the game never sees your payment details or your account. The game keeps on your device only that ads are
removed, and **Restore purchases** in Settings asks Google Play to bring it back on a new device.

Unity's In-App Purchasing, part of the game, passes the purchase between the game and Google Play. It sends Unity
Technologies a record each time a purchase starts, goes through, fails or is restored: the item, its price and
currency, and the store's receipt (its order number and signature, never your payment details); the game's version
and a random ID Unity makes for this installation; and the device's model, operating system version and language. It
may also contact Unity as the game starts, to fetch its own settings. It's sent encrypted, and Unity handles it under its
[Game Player and App User Privacy Policy](https://unity.com/legal/game-player-and-app-user-privacy-policy).

## The game engine

Two Rooms is made with Unity, and uses no Unity Ads or Unity Analytics. The Unity engine may send Unity Technologies
limited technical information about the device, such as its model and operating system; it includes nothing you do in
the game. Unity's [Game Player and App User Privacy Policy](https://unity.com/legal/game-player-and-app-user-privacy-policy)
explains what Unity collects and why.

## Permissions

The game asks Android for: internet access and network state, used by the ads, by buying and by downloading chapters;
billing, so Remove ads can be bought through Google Play; vibration, for a buzz when you find something (it can be
turned off in Settings); and permissions Google's ad library adds for its work: the advertising ID, Android's ad
services (topics and attribution), keeping the device awake for a moment (wake lock) and a short task while the game is
in the background (foreground service). The game has no access to your location, contacts, camera, microphone, photos
or files.

## Children

Two Rooms is a murder mystery for players 13 and over, and is not directed at children under 13. We don't knowingly
collect information from children. If you believe a child under 13 has used the game in a way that shared their
information, email us and we'll help.

## Your rights

Oasis Forge makes the game and is responsible for this policy. What's on your device is yours to delete, and we hold
nothing about you. What Google and Unity hold for ads and purchases is covered above. Where your country's law gives you
rights over your data (such as the GDPR in Europe and the UK), you can ask us about it, and about how to reach Google and
Unity for what they hold, by emailing us; we answer within a month. You can also complain to your country's data
protection authority.

Why data is used: ads (consent where the law asks for it, otherwise our legitimate interest in paying for a free game)
and the purchase (to give you what you bought). Google and Unity may handle data in the United States and other
countries, under their own safeguards. For how long they keep it, see Google's
[retention page](https://policies.google.com/technologies/retention) and the retention part of Unity's
[Game Player and App User Privacy Policy](https://unity.com/legal/game-player-and-app-user-privacy-policy).
